Questions people actually ask

Straight answers, including the awkward ones. If your question isn't here, send it over.

The guarantee

What exactly is the guarantee?

25% ad spend reduction in 30 days, scaling toward 50% by day 60–90 — or you don't pay.

Available to accounts spending $10K+/month on Amazon ads, after a qualifying audit. If we miss the number, you get your money back or we keep working free until we hit it. Your choice.

Why isn't the guarantee offered to everyone?

Because it wouldn't be honest. The reduction comes from waste that's already in the account. Under $10K/month in ad spend there usually isn't enough of it to promise a number against.

The audit is where we find out whether your account has that room. If it doesn't, we'll tell you, and we won't offer the guarantee.

Won't cutting ad spend cut my sales too?

That's the right thing to be worried about, and it's why the target is a spend reduction, not a spend cap.

The reduction comes from paying less per click for the same clicks — not from buying fewer of them. Look at Zestt Organics on the results page: spend down 40%, revenue up from $50K to $110K/month. If revenue starts dropping, the bid strategy is wrong and we fix it.

Isn't a 50% reduction a bit much to claim?

It's the upper end and it takes 60 to 90 days. The number we commit to at 30 days is 25%. We lead with the 25% for that reason.

Can you guarantee my sales will go up?

No, and be careful with anyone who does. Nobody can promise a sales number on Amazon without using tactics that break Amazon's Terms of Service. Too much of it — demand, competition, your inventory, your margins — sits outside what an agency controls.

What we can guarantee is what you pay per click, because that's genuinely within our control. That's why the guarantee is written against ad spend reduction and not against revenue.

What we commit to beyond that is picking opportunities carefully and putting real expertise behind them. We won't take you on at all if we don't think we can move the number.

Pricing and terms

How does pricing work?

A flat monthly fee, quoted after the audit once we've seen the size and state of the account. It never scales with your ad spend.

We quote on the call, not before — quoting blind means either overcharging small accounts or underquoting complicated ones.

Why not charge a percentage of ad spend like everyone else?

Because it points the agency the wrong direction. Under that model, an agency that halves your ad spend halves its own revenue. Nobody does that on purpose.

Is there a long contract?

Terms are covered on the audit call and put in writing before anything starts. There's no hidden auto-renewal.

Why do you charge so much?

We think we're competitively priced for what's involved, and we're picky about who we take on precisely so the fee is obviously worth it.

We're not looking for short-term revenue from clients we can't help. The whole model depends on long relationships, which only happen when the value is clear enough that you don't think twice about the invoice.

We have a lot of SKUs and can't afford your rates

Tell us anyway. If we look at the business and think we can add real value, we'll negotiate. Every situation is different and we'd rather build an agreement that fits than lose a good account over a rate card.

Scope

Do you handle Walmart, eBay, or Shopify?

We've worked across other e-commerce channels, but Amazon is the focus and that's deliberate. If your situation calls for something broader, ask — we'll tell you honestly whether we're the right people for it.

Do you only do PPC?

No. PPC is what we're known for and it's where the guarantee applies, but we're a full-service Amazon agency — graphics, keyword and content optimization, A+ content, 3D rendering, account troubleshooting, product vetting, A/B testing, inventory management, and IP enforcement.

How many clients do you have?

We don't publish numbers, but the roster is deliberately small. We don't overextend — when we're at capacity we stop taking clients until there's room, rather than stretching thinner and calling it growth.

The audit call

What happens on the call?

Twenty minutes. You share your screen or grant read access, Dale opens the account, and walks through what he sees: bid settings, placement multipliers, campaign structure, where spend is going that isn't producing anything.

You end the call knowing what you'd change even if you change it yourself.

Is this actually free, or is it a sales call?

It's free, and it's also how we decide whether to work with you. Both things are true and pretending otherwise would be silly.

What it isn't: a slide deck, a discovery call that ends in "let me schedule a follow-up," or someone reading you a generic report. Dale is looking at your live account.

Do I have to give you access to my Seller Central account?

Not for the audit — screen sharing is fine. If we work together, we'll ask for standard user permissions on the advertising side only.

Why do you ask about my ad spend before I can book?

Because Dale takes every call personally and there are a limited number of them. The $10K/month floor is where the method has enough to work with.

If you're below it, that's not a no forever. Take a look at everything else we do — content, graphics, 3D, inventory, IP enforcement — most of it doesn't have a spend floor.

How the work runs

Who will be my main point of contact?

Dale. He's the founder, he runs the audit, and he manages the account personally from there. Not a junior, not a rotating pod, not an offshore team behind an account manager's inbox.

That's the single thing most clients tell us they were missing somewhere else.

How fast do you respond? What are your working hours?

Within 24 hours, every time.

We don't keep fixed hours — the team is spread across time zones, so things tend to move while you're asleep. What we commit to is the 24-hour response, not a 9-to-5.

How long until I see results?

Spend reduction shows up inside the first 30 days, because turning off dynamic bidding and placement multipliers has an immediate effect. Structural work — rebuilding campaigns for your current scale — takes longer and is where the compounding happens.

I already have an agency. Is switching a nightmare?

It's a permissions change, not a migration. Nothing has to be rebuilt to hand it over, and campaign history stays intact.

Worth doing the audit before you fire anyone. If your current setup is in good shape, you'll find that out.

Do you work outside Amazon?

No. Amazon PPC only. That focus is the point.
